# Getting started

Ostler is a project manager that keeps everything on your computer unless you choose to connect or
share it. There is no account to create and no tracking. You plan projects with tasks, boards,
lists, calendars and timelines, and keep your documents next to the work.

## The welcome

The first time you open Ostler, a short welcome helps you set up:

1. **Your name and theme.** Tell Ostler what to call you. Tasks you assign to yourself show this
   name. Pick Light, Dark, or System to follow Windows. You see each theme as you choose it.
2. **Your first project.** Start from a template (you choose which on the next step), start a blank
   project, or import a spreadsheet (CSV) or a Trello board.
3. **Optional extras.** Link a folder if the project is software, set up AI help, and, once a folder is linked, turn on AI
   agents. Each one can be skipped and done later in Settings.

Just want to look around? Below a divider on the second screen, **Start with the demo projects**
adds both demos (a simple one and an advanced one) and opens the simple one straight away,
skipping the rest of the welcome. A bar at the top shows how far along the welcome you are.

When you finish, Ostler opens your new project. **Skip for now** is always there: before your
first project is made, it closes the welcome and creates nothing.

## The sample project

"Welcome to Ostler (Demo Project)" is a weekend away for you and three made-up friends, Sam, Priya
and Jordan (marked "(demo)", in the project's own People). Read its To do column from top to bottom. The first
tasks use the everyday tools; the later ones show milestones, the Timeline, decisions, goals,
costs, the shopping list and more. Each task says what
it shows, and the Read me note in its Library explains the rest. Open it any time from the
command palette (`Ctrl` + `K`, then "Open the sample project"). It is a project like any other, so
remove it when you are done.

The first time it opens, a note offers a short tour of it, and a few tips point things out as you
look around. [Tips and tours](26-tips-and-tours.md) says how to take the tour again or turn tips
off.

## The advanced demo

"Harbour Café refit (Advanced Demo)" picks up where the sample project stops. A small studio (you,
Leah, Tom and Ade) refits a café for its owner, Marta. Everyone in it is made up and marked
"(demo)". It has no folder and nothing to do with software, and it shows the tools for bigger jobs:

- every Board column, each renamed for the job (Waiting on someone, Client sign-off);
- two [workstreams](31-workstreams.md), Building work and Front of house, and
  [saved views](09-search-filters-and-saved-views.md) of the Board, List and Timeline: the Board
  in a lane per workstream, Front of house with the columns it doesn't use hidden, snags by room,
  Tom's open work, orders to chase and who's on what;
- weekly [iterations](23-iterations.md), one finished and one under way;
- [time tracking](20-time-tracking.md) with an hourly rate, and a week already invoiced;
- costs against a budget, a shopping list, and suppliers in [Contacts](25-contacts.md);
- [rules](24-rules.md) that act on their own, and task types with their own fields and templates;
- tasks that wait with a lag, and a baseline on the Timeline showing how the dates have moved;
- status updates and goal check-ins on the Overview, won't-do tasks, links between tasks and the
  Matrix.

Its dates count from the day you open it, so it always looks as if the work is under way. The Read
me note in its Library says where to find each part. The two demo projects always come together:
the welcome, New project and Home add both, and the command palette opens either ("Open the
sample project", "Open the advanced demo project"), adding whichever is missing.

When you are done with them, **Remove the demo projects…** removes both at once: it is in either
demo's menu, its Project settings › Remove, and the command palette, and Home offers it once you
have a project of your own ("Done with the demos?"; close the offer to keep them). It asks once,
with no name to type, and says if you added tasks or comments to them. Their made-up people go
with them; your address book is left alone. Bring them back the same way
as before. A copy of a demo opened from a backup or a project file is an ordinary project and is
removed like one.

## The getting started checklist

Home shows a short checklist until you have tried the basics:

- Create a task
- Give a task a due date
- Try another view: List, Calendar or Timeline
- Find something with the command palette (`Ctrl` + `K`)
- See where your backups are
- Set up AI (optional)

Each step ticks itself off when you do it, wherever you do it. Tasks that came from a template,
the sample project or an import don't count: make one of your own. Close the checklist with its X
button if you don't need it.

## Finding your way around

- The **sidebar** on the left holds Home, Inbox, My work, your projects, Settings and Help.
- Each **project** opens with its everyday views as tabs along the top: Overview, Board, List,
  Calendar and Library. **More views**, after the tabs, lists the rest with a line on what each is
  for, and a view the project uses earns a tab of its own ([Views](04-views.md)).
- **Settings** › General shows each group on one line with what it is set to now. Click a line to
  open it in place; a group you leave open is open next time too.
- The **command palette** (`Ctrl` + `K`) finds any project, view, setting, task, comment,
  decision or Library note.
- Press `?` to see every keyboard shortcut.
- The **menu button** at the top left of the window opens the menus: File, Edit, View, Go and
  Help. The keys shown beside each item are the same keyboard shortcuts. The top strip also shows
  where you are, and you can drag the window by it. To use Windows' own title bar and menu bar
  instead, turn on Settings › General › **Use the standard Windows title bar** and restart Ostler.
- Right-click Ostler on the taskbar for **New task**, **Inbox** and your recent projects.
- Links that start with `ostler://` open Ostler at the Inbox, Quick add, a project's view,
  a task, a milestone, a decision or a Library item. Notifications use them, and so can your own
  notes and shortcuts. Inside Ostler, type `[[` to make one ([Links in text](03-tasks.md#links-in-text)).

## Language

Settings › General › **Language** sets the language Ostler is shown in. **Match Windows**, the
default, uses your Windows display language when Ostler has it, and English otherwise. A change takes
effect at once, menus and notifications included. Dates, times and numbers always follow your
Windows region, whatever the language.

Ostler is in English for now. Translations come from people who use it: if you would like Ostler in
your language, [Translating Ostler](https://github.com/AntonyWiles/ostler/blob/master/docs/translating.md)
shows how to help.

## Keeping Ostler up to date

The installed Ostler looks for a new version when it starts and downloads it in the background.
When it is ready, you get a message and an Inbox item: **Update ready. Restart to update**. Ostler
never restarts on its own. If agents are working, choose **Restart when agents finish** in
Settings › About, where you can also check now, see what's new in the update, stop automatic checks
or downloads, and get beta versions early. Uninstalling keeps your data unless you tick **Also
remove my Ostler data**. A portable Ostler, built from source with `npm run package:portable`, does
not update itself.
