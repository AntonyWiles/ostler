# Whiteboards

A whiteboard is a page with no edges for thinking a project through: sticky notes, text, shapes,
arrows, drawings, pictures and mind maps, laid out however you like, next to the plan. When the
ideas settle, turn notes into tasks in a click.

Whiteboards live in the **Plan** view (see
[Milestones and goals](06-milestones-and-goals.md#plan-a-project)): **Whiteboards** sits below the
steps and lists the project's boards, newest first. Each board is also an item in the project's
[Library](07-library.md), in its **Whiteboards** section. A board belongs to its project, so on a
[shared project](28-sharing-a-project.md) everyone sees the same boards, and they are kept in step
on [your devices](29-your-devices.md).

## Making one

Press **New whiteboard**, in the Plan's **Whiteboards** or in the Library's **Add** menu. Give it a
**Title** (Ostler suggests "Whiteboard 1", "Whiteboard 2" and so on), choose what to **Start from**,
and press **Create**:

- **Blank**: an empty board.
- **Brainstorm**: a mind map around your project, with three ideas to get going.
- **Retrospective**: what went well, what could be better and what you'll try next.
- **Project kick-off**: the goal, who it's for, what's in and out, the risks and the next steps.
- **Journey map**: five stages, with what someone does, thinks and feels at each.

The board opens over the whole page, with **Back to the plan** to close it and **Rename** to change
its title. Ostler saves as you work, and the status bar at the bottom of the window says **Saved**
quietly (or **Saving**, when a save takes more than a moment). If a save fails it says **Couldn't
save: retrying** there, and above the board, and keeps trying.

## Moving around

- **Pan**: scroll with the mouse wheel or touchpad (`Shift` with the wheel goes sideways), drag with
  the **Hand** tool (`H`), or hold `Space` and drag with any tool.
- **Zoom**: `Ctrl` with the wheel, or pinch on a touchpad, zooms where the pointer is. `+` and `-`
  zoom in and out, `Shift` + `1` fits the whole board, and `Shift` + `0` goes back to 100%, as does
  the zoom level button. A board opens fitted to what is on it.
- **Select**: with **Select** (`V`), click an object, `Shift`-click to add more, or drag a box
  around several. `Ctrl` + `A` selects everything and `Escape` nothing.
- **Move and resize**: drag the selection, or drag a handle to resize; `Shift` keeps the shape as
  you resize. From the keyboard, the arrow keys move the selection and `Alt` with the arrow keys
  resizes one object; add `Shift` to go 10 times as far.

Objects snap to a grid as you place, move and resize them. Hold `Alt` while you drag to place them
freely.

Right-click the board or the selection, or press `Shift` + `F10`, for what you can do there: add a
note, text or mind map where you clicked, edit, link, connect, cut, copy, paste, duplicate, arrange,
group and delete.

A whiteboard you leave open on the Plan is open again when you come back to the Plan from another
view, until you press **Back to the plan**.

`Delete` deletes the selection. `Ctrl` + `Z` undoes and `Ctrl` + `Y` redoes, on the board or with
the **Undo** and **Redo** buttons at the board's lower right. The board's changes are in the
project's [Undo history](27-undo-history.md) too.

## Notes, text, shapes and frames

- **Sticky note** (`N`), drawn as a paper note with a folded corner, and **Text** (`T`): click on the board, or press `Enter` to place one in the
  middle of the view, and type. `Escape` or `Ctrl` + `Enter` finishes; a new text left empty goes
  away again.
- **Shape** (`S`): click to place, or drag to draw it; hold `Shift` for a square or a circle. Press
  `S` again to move on to the next shape: rectangle, rounded rectangle, ellipse, diamond and
  triangle.
- **Frame** (`F`): a tinted area with a title tab, for grouping a part of the board. Frames stay
  behind everything else, and moving a frame moves what is inside it.

Double-click an object, or select it and press `Enter`, to change its words or a frame's title.

The **Style** controls sit in the bar along the top of the board, always in the same place and
order: **Colour**, **Text size**, **Alignment**, **Shape**, **Fill**, **Line**, **Arrow heads**,
**Dashed** and **Line width**. Each shows what is set now, and opens a short list (or for
**Colour**, ten swatches) to change it. A change applies to everything selected. A control that
doesn't fit what you selected is greyed out and says why when you point at it.

With nothing selected, the controls set what the chosen tool makes next. Each drawing tool keeps its
own colour, shown as a dot on its button in the tool bar: the sticky note yellow and the pen black,
for example, until you change them. The tools are in groups: selecting and moving, things with
words, shapes and lines, then drawing.

**Arrange** puts the selection in front of or behind other objects (**Bring to front**, `Ctrl` +
`Shift` + `]`; **Bring forward**, `Ctrl` + `]`; **Send backward**, `Ctrl` + `[`; **Send to back**,
`Ctrl` + `Shift` + `[`), lines objects up (**Align left**, **Align centres** and the rest, for two
or more) and spaces them evenly (**Distribute horizontally** or **vertically**, for three or more).
**Group** (`Ctrl` + `G`, or the button after the style controls) makes objects select and move
together; **Ungroup** is `Ctrl` + `Shift` + `G`. A selected group shows a dashed outline with a
**Group** tag. To pick one object in a group, `Ctrl`-click it; dragging it then moves only that
object.

`Ctrl` + `D` duplicates the selection. `Ctrl` + `C`, `Ctrl` + `X` and `Ctrl` + `V` copy, cut and
paste objects, on the same board or another one, and each paste lands a little further on. Pictures
are left out when you paste into another project's board. Pasted into another app, copied objects
are their words, one line each.

## Arrows and lines

Choose **Line or arrow** (`A`) and drag from one object to another. The object a line will stick to
is outlined as you drag; a line's stuck ends follow their objects when you move them, and a loose
end stays where you left it. Select a line to drag either end to another object.

A line stays selected after you draw it, so the **Line**, **Arrow heads** and other controls change
it straight away. To bend a line, select it and drag one of the round handles halfway along it: that
adds a bend point, shown as a small diamond. Drag a bend point to move it, and double-click it to
take it away. Right-click a line for **Add a bend point here**, **Remove this bend point** and
**Straighten the line**. An **Elbow** line turns its corners on the way through its bend points.

Double-click a line, or select it and press `Enter`, to give it a label. The **Style** bar sets its
**Colour**, **Line** (**Straight** or **Elbow**), **Arrow heads** (**None**, **At the end**, **At
the start** or **Both**), **Dashed** and **Line width**.

**Node** (`J`) shows a small circle, a node point, in the middle of each side of every object.
Drag from one node point to another: the node your line will join lights up as you get near it, and
the line stays pinned to those two points when the objects move. Dragging a line's end with Select
shows the node points too, so you can pin an existing line the same way. The **Style** bar sets the
lines the node tool draws, as it does for **Line or arrow**.

To connect without dragging, select an object and press `C`, or **Connect to…** after the style
controls, then choose the other object from the list (type in **Filter** to find it) and press
**Connect**.

## Drawing

- **Pen** (`P`) draws freehand.
- **Marker** (`M`) draws a wider stroke, for highlighting.
- **Eraser** (`E`) rubs out a drawing it touches, whole.

While the pen or marker is chosen, the **Style** bar sets the colour and line width of the next
stroke. Very long strokes are smoothed a little as they are saved.

With any drawing tool (notes, text, mind maps, shapes, frames, the pen and the marker), point at the
edge of an object: the pointer shows a move cross, and dragging there moves the object without going
back to Select.

## Pictures

Press **Insert a picture** (`I`) and choose **From a file…** or **From the Library…**, or paste a
picture, or drop picture files on the board. Pictures are placed at a size that fits on the
board, never larger than they are.

Every picture on a board is kept in the project's Library, like any picture you add there: a
picture from a file, a paste or a drop is added to the Library first, and one already in the
Library is used as it is. Only pictures go on a whiteboard; other files you drop are left out, and
Ostler says how many.

## Pasting text and links

What you paste becomes what fits it:

- A link to a task, milestone, decision or Library item becomes a card for that item.
- A web address becomes a note linked to that page.
- Any other text becomes one note.

`Ctrl` + `Shift` + `V` pastes each line as its own note, up to 50 lines at a time. Ostler says how
many lines it left out.

## Links and item cards

Any object except lines and drawings can link to a task, milestone, decision, Library item or web
page. Select it and press **Link to an item or web page** (`L`). The dialog lists the project's
tasks, milestones, decisions and Library items straight away, newest first; **Show** narrows the
list to one kind, or to **Cloud files**: Library links to OneDrive, SharePoint, Google Drive or
Dropbox files, each marked with its service. Type to search, or type a **Web address**, and press
**Link**. **Remove link** in the same dialog takes it off.

To link a cloud file, first add it to the Library as a link.

With nothing selected, the same tool adds a card: a card shows a task, milestone, decision or
Library item, with its title and status kept up to date.

A linked object shows a small badge in its corner, with a task's status. To open a link, click the
badge, double-click a card, or select the object and press `O`. A task opens in its drawer with the
board still open under it; a web address opens in your browser. A link to an item that has since
been deleted shows a grey badge, and opening it says **Deleted item**.

## Making tasks

Select notes, shapes or mind-map ideas and press **Make tasks** after the style controls, or `K`. Each
one becomes a task, read as [quick add](03-tasks.md) reads it: the first line is the title, so
`#label`, `@person`, `!high` and dates such as `friday` work, and the rest is the description.

The dialog shows the tasks before anything is made, and what it left out and why: objects already
linked, objects with no words, and objects that aren't notes, shapes or mind-map ideas. It makes at
most 50 tasks at a time. Choose a **Milestone** (a planned or active one) or **No milestone**, and
press **Make 3 tasks**, for example.

The tasks are made together, each lists the whiteboard as its source, and each object links to its
task, so its badge shows the task's status from then on. One **Undo** in the message removes the
tasks and their links, even after you have closed the board.

## Mind maps

A mind map is a central idea with ideas branching out to the right, each joined to its parent by a
curved branch. The central idea is a solid pill; the other ideas are lighter pills.

Choose **Mind map** (`B`) and click, or press `Enter`, to place a central idea, and type.

**While typing an idea:**

- `Tab` adds a child idea.
- `Enter` adds the next idea beside it.
- `Shift` + `Enter` starts a new line in the idea.

**With an idea selected:**

- `Insert` adds a child idea.
- The arrow keys move along the map's branches.
- `Enter` changes its words, and `Delete` deletes it with its branch.
- `Shift` with the arrow keys moves it with its branch; on the central idea, the whole map.

The layout is automatic: the map grows to the right of its central idea, each idea sized to its
words, and it is laid out again after every change. Drag the central idea to move the whole map.
Drag any other idea to put it where you like: its branch comes with it, its branches follow as you
drag, and the layout leaves it there from then on. Right-click it and choose **Put back in the map's
layout** to undo that. After you change the central idea's colour, **Apply to the whole map** in
the **Colour** list gives every idea that colour.

## Export, import and print

The open board's **Export** menu has:

- **PNG picture** and **SVG drawing**: the whole board as a picture.
- **Whiteboard file**: a `.whiteboard.json` file with the board and its pictures, to bring into
  Ostler on another PC or project.
- **Print…** and **Save as PDF…**: the whole board fitted to the page's width, under its title
  and the date it was printed. A very tall board runs over several pages.

Ostler saves the board before it exports or prints. A picture that isn't on this PC is left out
of a whiteboard file, and Ostler says how many; a PNG or SVG shows a missing-picture box in its
place.

To bring a whiteboard file in, press **New whiteboard**, then **Import a whiteboard file…**. The
board is added with its title, and its pictures go into the Library. Ostler checks the file first
and refuses one it can't read, adding nothing.

## Working together

On a [shared project](28-sharing-a-project.md), a Viewer sees every board but can't change it, and
an archived board is read-only for everyone. Both can still be exported and printed.

Changes made on another PC, or in another window, appear on an open board as they arrive, and
yours go to them. When they arrive, the board's undo starts afresh.

## Ostler AI and coding agents

A whiteboard's text, for search, [Ostler AI](13-ostler-ai.md) and agents, is an outline Ostler
writes from the board: the words of its notes, text, shapes and cards, frame by frame in reading
order, its mind maps as lists, which objects its lines and arrows connect, and each object's link.
Positions and colours aren't in it, and pictures are only named.

A new whiteboard's Ostler AI access is **When selected**: Ostler AI reads it when you choose it,
or when it is attached to a task. Attach a board to a task (see [Library](07-library.md#attach-to-tasks))
and an [agent](14-agents.md) working on that task reads its outline too.

## Project files and templates

An exported [project file](11-import-and-export.md) carries the project's whiteboards with their
pictures, and importing it brings them back, with their links pointing at the imported items. A
project [template](10-templates.md) carries its whiteboards too, without their pictures.

## Using a keyboard and a screen reader

Every tool and action has a key; [Keyboard shortcuts](16-keyboard-shortcuts.md#whiteboards) lists
them all. Single-letter keys work when you're not typing in a text box.

- `Tab` and `Shift` + `Tab` move from object to object in reading order, top to bottom and left to
  right. When you reach the board, its description says how to move, resize and delete.
- **Skip past the whiteboard** jumps over the board to what comes after it.
- `Enter` places the chosen tool's object in the middle of the view, so you can build a board
  without a pointer, and **Connect to…** makes lines from a list.
- Each object is read by its kind and words, such as "Note: Call the venue", with its link and a
  task's status. Mind-map ideas also say where they are, such as "Idea: Venue, level 1, 1 of 2".
  Objects that would read alike are numbered in reading order, such as "Rectangle 2", here and in
  **Connect to…**.
- Ostler announces what changes: what was added, moved, deleted, grouped, pasted and the zoom.

Freehand drawing with the pen, marker and eraser needs a mouse, pen or touch.

## Limits

- 5,000 objects on a board.
- 10,000 characters of text in one object.
- 2,000 points in one drawing; a longer stroke is simplified to fit.
- 2,000 characters in a link.
- 50 tasks from one **Make tasks**, and 50 notes from one `Ctrl` + `Shift` + `V`.
- Zoom from 10% to 400%.
- A whiteboard file of at most 100 MB, with at most 200 pictures.
- An outline of at most 200,000 characters; a board with more text is cut short there, and the
  outline says so.
